\section{Method}
In this section the various methods utilized are presented. 

\subsection{Integrating Factor}

For the general PDE, $u_t = \mathcal{L}u + \mathcal{N}\left(u,t\right)$, multiplication by the exponetial $ e^{-Lt} $ reduces the problem to:
\begin{align*}
    v_t &= e^{-Lt}\mathcal{N}\left(e^{Lt}v\right) \\
    v   &= e^{-Lt}u
\end{align*}

For purely linear problems this method gives an explicit solution
\begin{equation}
    u\left(x,t\right) = u\left(x,0\right)e^{Lt},
\end{equation}

That can be effectivly computed using matrix products. 

In the general case with \textit{Nonlinearities} one can use Runge-Kutta or other time-stepping methods.
Based on the paper from Kassam and Trefethen \cite{ETD}, ETD methods seems to work quite well. 

\subsection{Exponentital Time Differention}
By integrating the IF formula over a $single$ time step $h$ we get
\begin{equation}
    u_{n+1} = e^{Lh}u_n + e^{Lh} \int_{0}^h e^{-L\tau} \mathcal{N}\left( u(t_n + \tau), t_n + \tau \right).
\end{equation}

An exact solution! All that is left is to approximate the integral (see Cox and Matthews)
